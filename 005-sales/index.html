Overview

This page organizes the sales role around the actual workday — what to check, what to do at each stage of an appointment, and what to say to a stale lead — rather than the full CRM bucket-by-bucket reference. For automation detail, exact triggers, and open questions on any status below, see the CRM Playbook's stage notes.

Where a Sales Rep Owns the Pipeline

The Short Version

A rep's ownership starts once a lead is Lead Set (appointment booked) and runs through Job Sold — confirming the appointment, running the demo, sending the estimate, chasing nudges, and co-signing the contract. Everything before Lead Set (first-touch calling, speed-to-lead) is lead management; everything after Job Sold (deposit, ordering, install, cash-out) is production/admin. Reps still show up in a few of those later steps (the deposit to-do, install-day contact) but don't own the process end to end.

Daily Standard Guidelines

The habits that keep a rep's book of appointments and follow-ups from going stale. Full To-Dos SOP is in the CRM Playbook — condensed here to what applies day-to-day.

Review Twice a Day

Check your To-Do list morning and afternoon. Overdue and due-today items sort to the top — clear those first.

Phone + Text First

Primary contact channels are phone and text. Email is optional — fine as a personal touch, but not the standard channel, and we don't always know if a client's number is mobile so text isn't guaranteed to land either.

Every Follow-Up Is a To-Do

Create a To-Do for every call, follow-up, and next step tied to a customer — never rely on memory or the Call Queue (that's front-desk-only). Label it "📞 Call [Customer Name] – [Purpose]" so it's scannable at a glance.

Log the Real Content

After any contact attempt, log it under Client Activity — and write what was actually said, not just "left voicemail." If a follow-up is still needed, push the due date out instead of closing the To-Do.

Nothing Sits for a Day

No lead or appointment should sit untouched for more than a day. If you're waiting on the client, that's still a scheduled follow-up To-Do — not a blank spot on your list.

Appointment Day Checklist

From Lead Set through Demo — what to actually do at each point, in order.

Before: Lead Set → Confirmed & Issued

☐ Once the appointment shows as Lead Set, confirm it with the client yourself — the CRM only auto-advances to Lead Confirmed & Issued once you mark it confirmed.

☐ Send brochures per the auto-created To-Do (fires 1 minute after status is set).

☐ Watch for the reminder SMS that goes to you and the client 1 hour before the meeting.

Day Of: Arrival

☐ If the client isn't there or doesn't answer: mark it No Show/Reset, document the reason, and create a To-Do to attempt rebooking.

☐ If you meet but the job is outside scope: mark it No Demo — no quote issued, no further automation.

☐ If the demo happens: starting the project/estimate in the client profile is what auto-moves the lead to Demo — do this live or immediately after.

Right After: Estimate

☐ Send the estimate — this triggers an immediate SMS follow-up plus the automated nudge sequence.

☐ Don't rely on nudges alone — a personal call between automated touches is still expected, especially for larger jobs.

Closing the Sale

Estimate Sent through Job Sold — the manual checkpoints that are easy to let slip because most of this stage looks automated.

Estimate Sent — Nudges

3 automated SMS nudges + 2 email nudges fire over the following days. Auto-advances to Demo/No Sale – Rehash after 31 days if it hasn't sold — so don't wait on the automation to force a decision, work it before day 31.

E-Signature Sent

☐ Send an immediate personal follow-up text once the e-sig goes out — the CRM already does one, but a personal touch closes faster.

☐ Watch the expiry To-Do — if the client doesn't sign in time it auto-reverts to Pending.

☐ Once the client signs, you'll get an email to co-sign — this is a manual step only you can do, and the contract isn't officially sold until you complete it. The CRM sends the signed copy to both sides only after your signature.

Job Sold

☐ Set the lead status and the project status separately — there's no automation linking them, they don't fire together.

☐ Deposit invoice: a To-Do goes to admin (Tristan/Tanya) automatically, but confirm it actually went out — this is where a sold job can quietly stall before production even starts.

Stale Lead Quick Reference (L-A-D-R)

Same process as the full script in the CRM Playbook — condensed here since reps use this directly on their own Rehash and Demo/No Sale leads.

Locate & Assess

Leads inactive 1–2 months+ that have already had all automated follow-ups. Confirm no estimate was scheduled and there's been no recent response before you call.

Do

Call first, then text if no answer. Goal is to find out if the project was completed elsewhere, postponed, or still needs us. If they're not ready, ask what's holding them back and get a follow-up date — create the To-Do on the spot.

Record

Note what the voicemail or text actually said — "Called and left voicemail" on its own isn't enough for the next person picking up the file.

Week 1 — Learn the System

☐ Shadow a confirmed appointment through to demo with an existing rep.

☐ Walk through the full pipeline glossary and stage map in the CRM Playbook — every bucket a lead can sit in, in order.

☐ Review this page's Daily Standard Guidelines and Appointment Day Checklist before taking a solo appointment.

☐ Confirm To-Do naming convention and logging habits with a manager before your first solo call.

Week 2 — Take Ownership

☐ Run a solo appointment start to finish, using the Appointment Day and Closing the Sale checklists as a live reference.

☐ Practice the L-A-D-R rehash script on an existing stale lead with a manager listening in.

☐ Review your open To-Do list twice daily unsupervised for a full week before it's considered habit.
