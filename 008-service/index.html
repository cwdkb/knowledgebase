Service Call Routing

How an incoming service call gets taken, qualified, and handed to the Service Department — three source docs cover this from slightly different angles (see the flag in the last card below).

Admin Intake — Taking the Call

The call is taken at the Admin level. Identify: client name, address, number, email address — pull the paper file for an old client if needed.

Warranty check: identify whether the issue is covered by CWD warranty and/or manufacturer warranty. Take clear details and pictures of the issue if necessary, and document everything in the client profile's notes and documents.

Hand-off: create a to-do to advise the Service Department.

Emergency service — a breach of security (e.g. door handle not locking, broken window or door) should be expedited and the home secured ASAP until the product is received and the work can be done.

Service Call Qualification Process Map Draft

Generation sources: website inquiries, phone calls, walk-ins, social media, referrals.

Initial contact: Customer Service Representative responds to the request within 24 hours by phone, email, or text.

Issue assessment questions: what's the problem with the windows/doors/siding, when was it first noticed, were there previous repairs/services, and what's the urgency?

Qualify against: service scope (within CWD's offerings), warranty status, and customer availability.

Then: schedule the appointment → Service Coordinator dispatches a briefed technician with the necessary tools/parts → technician diagnoses, repairs, and summarizes work done → Customer Service Rep follows up within 48 hours to confirm satisfaction → close the call (documentation complete, payment collected if applicable) → post-service follow-up to request a review.

KPIs tracked: response time to initial request, inquiry-to-appointment conversion rate, customer satisfaction/review ratings, resolution time.

Filing-location duplicate noted in Open Questions.

Two service categories: Installation Services — issues within 3 years of install, usually poor-craftsmanship-related; the original install crew is asked to return and repair, and if they decline, a "Charge Back Installer" (CBI) is issued for parts/labour to send a CWD service tech instead. Warranty Services — issues any time after install where the part failed through no fault of the customer; the manufacturer covers the part, CWD covers the labour.

Processing: gather info by phone/email/pictures → create a CWD Service Work Order under the original Opportunity → schedule an inspection if needed (photos/video, and determine installation-vs-warranty during the visit) → order parts from the manufacturer if needed → print the Work Order and place it in the correct physical bucket (Parts Received / Waiting for Parts / MFG Services) → log the customer name and brief description against the Work Order.

Bucket rules: monitored daily; ready-to-book services should be booked within a week of everything being ready; record order numbers next to the service; provide bi-weekly updates to customers waiting on parts. If a customer calls and nothing can be done immediately, tell them Service will follow up within 24–48 hours unless it's an emergency.

During/after the call: address listed items, note anything unfinished with a reason, communicate next steps to the customer before leaving, and get a customer sign-off (or document why one couldn't be obtained). If fully complete: mark finished, note under the Invoice tab, scan the signed Work Order, file the hard copy. If not complete: repeat with a new Work Order for the remaining work.

Chargeable Products

When a service inspection becomes chargeable, the current hourly/day rate sheet, and the inspection-fee process for Property Management and Insurance clients. See Open Questions for a rate discrepancy between two of the docs below.

Chargeable Inspection Policy — Admin Process

Principle: valid warranty-related inspections for CWD-covered products may be completed at no charge; if the issue is outside warranty scope, an inspection fee applies — including non-warranty requests, undocumented new issues, added-after-approval items, and any PM/Insurance assessment requiring formal reporting.

Approved fee schedule (distance-based): In-Town (Barrie) $125 · Within 30 KM $200 · 30–60 KM $300 · 60–90 KM $400 · 90–120 KM $500. Fees are billed as single-item service charges, must be disclosed before scheduling, and are non-refundable unless credited toward approved replacement work.

Credit rule: if replacement/corrective work proceeds through CWD and is signed/authorized, the inspection fee may be credited toward the project at management discretion. If no work proceeds, the fee remains payable and non-refundable.

No fee applies for valid active warranty claims, previously approved items already in an active scope of work, or follow-up visits tied to incomplete approved warranty work.

Quick reference: CWD product + warranty issue → no fee. CWD product + non-warranty issue → fee applies. New issue outside scope → fee applies. PM/Insurance assessment → fee applies. Replacement sold → fee credited. No sale → fee stays.

Roles: Service Coordinator/Admin verifies warranty history, communicates fees, documents scope, and schedules. Technician completes inspection/documentation. Management approves exceptions/credits. Accounting issues invoices and applies credits.

Chargeable Inspection Policy — Technician Process

Scope of work: technician may inspect/assess glass, hardware/handles, small repair conditions, and caulking — but must not perform full repairs or out-of-scope work.

Process: review the job file/notes before the visit and confirm product origin, reported issue, and that the fee was discussed → conduct the inspection, assessing cause (defect vs. misuse vs. environment), condition, and repair-vs-replacement recommendation → document findings, cause, and recommendations with clear photos → submit the inspection report with photos and the set/amended fee to both client and admin.

Technicians provide input if a quote or replacement is recommended, but do not communicate pricing unless instructed. Admin owns client communication, invoicing, and follow-up.

Inspection Fee Process — Property Management & Insurance Companies Different fee schedule

Applies to property management companies, insurance companies, realtors/third-party requests requiring a formal inspection report — and sometimes regular clients.

Fee schedule here doesn't match the Admin/Technician chargeable-inspection docs above — see Open Questions.

Fee & distance rules: $300 within 30 KM · $400 for 30–60 KM · $500 for 60–90 KM — added via the Inspection Fee category in the Price Book. If the client proceeds with a supply & install purchase, the full inspection fee is credited back on their final invoice.

Booking: confirm property address, distance, and scope → notify the client of the fee up front and confirm acceptance → create a Lead/Opportunity in Builder Prime (Interested in: Inspection/Quote, Lead Source, Lead Set By, assigned Salesrep, Class: Residential or PM/Insurance/Builder/Contractor) → schedule and assign the inspector/salesrep.

On-site: wide-angle + close-up photos of every issue, exterior contributing factors, observations (age/condition, product type, deficiencies, safety concerns), and measurements if replacement may be required.

Report: summary of issue/findings/severity/next steps, labeled photo documentation, and a replacement recommendation with reasoning if applicable.

If a quote is needed: create a new project under the same opportunity (change project type from Inspection to the appropriate type), build the estimate in DaVinci or the manufacturer's software, generate the contract in Builder Prime, and send — either through Builder Prime directly or as one combined email (report + photos + quote), updating Builder Prime either way.

Billing: create an "Inspection" project, generate the contract with the fee and terms, mark Sold in Builder Prime, and invoice immediately after sending the report — do not delay billing even if replacement may come later. Payments go to ar@completewd.com.

If the client proceeds with replacement: if the inspection fee was invoiced and paid, apply the credit to the replacement contract billing. If invoiced but not paid, credit the invoice instead (don't apply it to the replacement contract). If not yet invoiced, quote the replacement normally.

Service Request Process

The full numbered workflow (1–11) from SharePoint's Operations/Process Folder/OFFICE/TEMPLATES/Service Request Process — every doc in this series is filenamed "DRAFT HOW TO," so treat the whole thing as unconfirmed with Serge. See Open Questions for a duplicate to flag.

1. Intake & Processing (Front Administration)

Trigger: a client contacts the office by phone or email requesting service.

Collect: full name, phone, email, service address, description of issue, room location, product type, approximate install date. Request photos if applicable — full unit wide view, close-up of the issue, and the label/sticker if visible.

Set client expectations: send an acknowledgment email confirming the request was forwarded to Service, a Service rep will respond within 24–48 hours, and emergencies are escalated immediately.

Forward to Service: all collected details, a Builder Prime screenshot if available, all photos, and a clear written description. No request is considered complete until photos are received (unless it's an emergency).

2. Client Review & Setup (Service Coordinator) Duplicate exists

A near-duplicate of this step exists, written from Production's perspective — see Open Questions.

Trigger: Service Department receives a complete request from Front Administration.

Intake & file organization: move the email into the Pending Service folder, create a client-specific folder if needed, save all photos.

Review client history in Builder Prime: locate the client profile, select the correct Opportunity, and review the signed contract, warranty terms at time of sale, line items, and install date (or the invoice system for pre-Builder Prime jobs).

Determine service type: Manufacturer Service Warranty (product defect, sealed unit failure, frame defect — manufacturer supplies the part/service), CWD Service Warranty (installation-related, labour covered under company warranty), Rework Service (incomplete install, missed materials, adjustment from original install), or Chargeable Service (outside warranty — stress cracks, physical damage, wear & tear). Warranty determination must be completed before client follow-up.

If info is missing: contact the client for photos/video/measurements/clarification, and let them know the request is being reviewed and scheduling will follow once claim/parts/service requirements are confirmed.

Create the Work Order (mandatory for every service): select the Opportunity, create the Work Order, choose the Work Type (Manufacturer / CWD / Rework Service), and enter a description in the format "KV Service – Kitchen Window – Line 1 – Replace Sealed Unit – Stress Crack." Upload all service photos under Project → Photos → Service Photos and log that they were uploaded.

3. Parts Ordering

Trigger: a Service Work Order exists and review determines parts are required.

Identify the part: hardware, glass unit, frame component, screen, handle/lock, or other — using the client's description, photos, original order details, and manufacturer specs.

Check availability: if it's a stocked inventory item, retrieve it, attach it to the Work Order, and move straight to the Ready to Schedule bucket. If it's a special order, proceed to ordering.

Gather order info: manufacturer name, manufacturer order/SO number, original line item number, window/door location, colour, product type, defect description.

Submit the order: KV Windows orders go through Insight Pro (client info, order/line item numbers, purchase date, "Retailer Installed," issue description, photos). Other manufacturers (e.g. VinylBilt) are ordered by email with the same detail.

Document: paste the confirmation into the Builder Prime Work Order log, track the case in Insight Pro until a tech is assigned/part ships/service is scheduled, then print the Work Order and place it in the Waiting for Parts physical bucket (Builder Prime status stays "Unscheduled").

Verify the confirmation against what was submitted (double two-person check, signed off on the Work Order). If correct, staple it to the Work Order and record the expected ETA. If incorrect, notify the manufacturer within 24–48 hours, request a revision, and re-verify the corrected confirmation before re-attaching.

Mandatory client follow-up: confirm the claim was processed and the next step (manufacturer will contact directly / part ordered / we'll contact to schedule) — logged in Builder Prime.

4. Client Follow-Up Communication (Waiting for Part)

Purpose: maintain proactive communication while a part is on order, and reduce incoming follow-up calls.

Tracking: record expected ETA, manufacturer updates, and delays on the Work Order and internal tracking system.

Weekly review: a recurring reminder (example given: Tuesdays at 3:00 PM) to check all outstanding parts orders, ETA status, and delays, and determine what communication is due.

Communication cadence: initial touch when the request is reviewed and parts ordered; a secondary follow-up roughly 2 weeks later if still waiting; continued weekly updates if delays persist — all sent through Builder Prime's email system so the history stays visible to office staff.

Once parts arrive, communication shifts from a waiting update to a scheduling contact, and the process moves into Service Receiving & Scheduling.

5. Parts Receiving

Trigger: service parts arrive for an existing Work Order.

Receive & verify: match the packing slip to the correct Work Order, confirm the correct part/size/colour/configuration/line item with no visible damage or defects (visual inspection for sealed units, screens, larger items).

Document: attach the packing slip to the Work Order, alongside the order confirmation and original service request details.

Move forward: once verified, move the Work Order to the Ready to Schedule physical bucket. Incorrect, missing, or damaged parts must not move to scheduling.

6. Parts Scheduling & Tech Prep

Trigger: a Work Order has been verified and moved to Ready to Schedule.

Route planning: gather all Ready to Schedule Work Orders, group by geographic area, and estimate time (minor service 20–30 min, standard 1 hr, larger 1–2+ hrs), including travel and buffer time.

Contact the client: call with a pre-planned window, confirm the service request and appointment date/time, restate the scope of work, ask if there's anything else they'd like looked at, and confirm access details (someone home, lockbox, pets, etc.). Any additional items get added to the Work Order before scheduling.

Schedule in Builder Prime: assign the technician, set date and a start/end time window (default 1 hour, adjusted for longer services), save — the Work Order then appears on the Production Calendar.

Route/day structure: jobs scheduled in logical geographic order, typically 4–6 calls/day depending on scope. Current service days: Monday, Tuesday, and Friday (flex/overflow) — noted as subject to change with workload.

Technician docket: gather the day's Work Orders into a single service docket noting technician name, date, ordered list of calls, and any outstanding balance ("Client Last Name – Work Order # – Balance"), then place it in the technician's Scheduled Service bucket.

7. Work Preparation (Service Technician)

Trigger: scheduled Work Orders are in the technician's bucket and they're preparing to leave for the day.

Review: retrieve the full service docket, lay out all Work Orders, and review descriptions, coordinator notes, photos/video, and required repairs for each stop.

Verify parts: locate required parts across the service parts cabinet, warehouse, glass rack, or misc. service parts area, and confirm correct size, colour, configuration, and grill pattern against the Work Order.

Load & organize: load the truck, keep paperwork in stop order, and do a final pre-departure check (parts loaded, tools available, paperwork present).

If a part is missing, incorrect, or damaged: stop, notify the Service Coordinator, and jointly decide to proceed partially, reschedule, or reorder (looping back to Parts Ordering) — with the client informed if the plan changes. Emergency local-supplier purchases (company card preferred) are allowed only for minor/urgent items.

8. Onsite Execution & Client Sign-Off

Arrival: park appropriately, approach without tools initially, introduce yourself, and confirm the purpose of the visit.

Review the issue with the client to confirm scope matches the Work Order and catch any additional concerns before starting — then retrieve only the necessary tools/parts.

Prepare the work area (drop sheet/vacuum if needed) and follow footwear protocol for the home.

Execute the service, then clean the area and leave it in the same or better condition than found.

Close out: show the client the completed work, explain what was done, and obtain a signature on the service ticket confirming completion and satisfaction.

If service isn't fully completed (missing/incorrect part, additional issue found): inform the client onsite, explain next steps, then report back to the Service Coordinator with what was completed vs. outstanding — looping back into Parts Ordering if a new part is needed.

9. Work Order Logging & Close Out

Trigger: the technician returns to the office after completing scheduled calls.

Log every Work Order in Builder Prime's Log Tab with details of work completed, repairs performed, materials used, and any observations — every Work Order needs a log entry, no matter how minor.

If fully complete, mark the Work Order Complete and close it. If incomplete, document what's done vs. outstanding, then either create a new Work Order for the remaining items or notify the Service Coordinator directly (placing paperwork on their desk if unavailable).

Always close the original Work Order regardless of completion status — each site visit is tracked individually, and new scope always gets a new Work Order, never a modified old one.

Filing: completed Work Orders go into the Filing Holder (physical bucket) for Admin to file into the client file.

10. Re-Work & Follow-Up

Trigger: a service visit is done but needs further action — missing/incorrect parts, additional work found onsite, or incomplete tasks.

Route the follow-up: if no parts are needed (issue wasn't previously identified, part wasn't brought, minor follow-up), create a new Work Order and move directly to Ready to Schedule. If parts are needed (incorrect/missing/defective part, or a new issue needing a new part), create a new Work Order and return to the full Parts Ordering cycle.

Work Order management: the original Work Order is always closed as Complete, even if only partially done — a new Work Order carries the remaining scope, reason for follow-up, and any parts required, and becomes the active file going forward.

Handoff: ensure the Service Coordinator is aware, with the new Work Order and notes handed off or placed visibly if they're unavailable.

11. Work Order Scanning & Digital Filing

Trigger: completed Work Orders sit in the "Completed Work Orders" filing area, ready for processing.

Sort into two groups: with pricing (billable) and without pricing (warranty/no-charge/informational).

Scan each Work Order individually to your own email, then upload to Builder Prime: rename the file "WO ####" (Work Order number), search the Work Order number in BP, open the associated Project via the client's Work History, go to the Documents tab, and upload into a "Service Confirmations" folder (create it if missing).

Physical documents: Work Orders with pricing are filed in the job pouch/client file for financial record-keeping. Work Orders without pricing are shredded — but only after confirming the scan is uploaded, correctly named, and in the right folder. Never keep physical copies of non-billable Work Orders.
