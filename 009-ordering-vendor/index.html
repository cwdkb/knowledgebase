Ordering & Job Costing Chain

A five-doc sequence from SharePoint's Operations/Process Folder/OFFICE/TEMPLATES covering the path from a measured file landing in the "Ready to Order" bin through completed Builder Prime costing. Two of the five docs are both numbered "2" — see the flag below.

1. Initial File Review Prior to Ordering

Trigger: a measured project file is returned to the Production Office and placed in the physical Ready to Order bin, with Builder Prime status Ready to Order and Costed.

Owner: Production/Ordering Coordinator. Retrieves the file, verifies the remeasure pouch/docket, signed contract, file checklist sticker, and supporting documentation — confirming contract is signed (correct version), deposit collected (or an approved no-deposit exception, e.g. finance jobs, PM accounts), commission card completed, measure completed, and Builder Prime matches the physical file.

Stops the process (file returned for correction) if: signed contract missing, deposit not collected/unverifiable, remeasure documentation missing, or referenced plans/notes aren't physically included.

Measurement exceptions: new construction ordered from plans, or cash-and-carry using customer measurements — still require supporting documentation.

Remeasure pouch review: front cover checkboxes/ordering notes/site notes, plus the back for additional windows measured or site adjustments — all notes affecting ordering must be reviewed before continuing.

Once qualified, the file proceeds to manufacturer-specific ordering review (example given: the KV Windows process).

2. Measurement Prep for Ordering

Purpose: ensure measured products are accurately reviewed and updated in the manufacturer quoting software (example: KV Windows → DaVinci) before ordering or job costing begins.

Process: open the estimate by estimate/Builder Prime job number → update customer tracking fields (Customer Tag: Last Name, First Name; Customer PO: Last Name – Estimate Number) → review and update every line item individually against the final remeasure (measurements, swing direction, configuration, colours, accessories, install details).

Costing red flags: custom-sized doors sold under standard programs, significant sizing changes, configuration upgrades, major supplier cost increases. If a change significantly affects cost/profitability, stop ordering, place the contract on hold (Contract on Hold – Change Order Needed), and create a Builder Prime To-Do for the Sales Rep to decide whether to absorb the cost or go back to the client.

Special order items: while updating the estimate, also check the remeasure pouch/measure notes/contract for anything that must be ordered outside the main manufacturer order — if found, run the Special Order Items process before continuing.

Close-out: save the updated estimate, then generate the Dealer Estimate Detail Report — the internal pre-order cost confirmation used for Job Cost Breakdown calculations.

2. Special Order Items Also numbered "2"

This doc and "Measurement Prep for Ordering" are both filenamed as step 2 — likely a numbering slip in the draft series (see Open Questions).

Purpose: identify, order, document, and file any job-specific item required outside the main window/door manufacturer order, before the file moves into Job Costing. Covers special hardware, colour coil/core not in stock, coffee trim, colour-match caulking, shape trim, exterior shutters, storm doors, door handles, and other specialty items.

Ordering: most special items are ordered by email directly to the supplier (examples: Door Hardware Supply, Gentech, storm door/trim suppliers). PO format: Client Last Name + six-digit Builder Prime/CWD job number (e.g. SMITH 123456) in the email subject line.

Documentation: print the sent order email (acts as the internal PO until supplier confirmation arrives), label what it's for at the top (e.g. "Gentech PO"), place it in the job pouch, and record supplier/item/date/initials on the job docket's manufacturer/supplier section.

Template-dependent items (special/non-standard trim, e.g. from Simcoe Trim) can't be ordered until the main product arrives — flag for later ordering. Example cadence given: Simcoe Trim pickup is every Thursday; a template submitted before Thursday noon is normally ready the following Thursday — expect roughly a two-week delay from main-product arrival for template-required trim.

Receiving/labeling of special items belongs to the separate Receiving process, not this one. Once all immediately-orderable special items are ordered and documented, the file continues to Job Costing.

3. Job Costing — Excel Spreadsheet

Trigger: Measurement Prep for Ordering is complete and all immediate special order items have been ordered/documented.

Template rule: open the live Job Cost Breakdown template with auto-save OFF, then immediately Save As into Teams / Admin Management / Admin Entries / [current year] / Job Cost Breakdown [year]. The live template must never be overwritten.

File naming (all caps): LAST NAME – ESTIMATE NUMBER – MONTH YEAR (e.g. PATERNO 12345 JAN2026). Use both names if hyphenated; use the first-listed last name if two different surnames apply.

Labor Breakdown 1 tab: enter contract/estimate number, customer name, site address, then per line item — job type (Patio Door, Window, Entry Door, Siding, Aluminum), install type (5'/6' cap, new construction, no capping, specialty), quantity, width, height, trim type. Labor rate, linear trim footage, and square footage auto-calculate. Add extras (jam build-outs, re-blinds, travel charges, specialty labor) per item, then print the Labor Breakdown sheet for the job pouch.

Job Cost Breakdown tab: contract date, contract amount (post-tax), supplier (dropdown, e.g. KV), product material cost (pre-tax, pulled from the Dealer Estimate Detail Report), and sales representative — which auto-populates commission split. Print the final sheet, save/close, and collect all printed paperwork for the job file.

4. Job Costing — CRM (Builder Prime)

Trigger: the Job Cost Breakdown spreadsheet is completed and printed.

Labor: in the job's Scope Step → Labor and Materials subtask → Assignments tab, select Sub Cost (placeholder since the final crew isn't assigned yet), enter estimated effort hours (from the remeasure pouch) and labor cost (from the Job Cost Breakdown), then click Assign — the entry won't save otherwise.

Material: Materials tab → search vendor (format: "Vendor – Company Name") → enter product cost in the Quantity field (BP calculates dollar value from it) → Save and Close. Equipment charges are left blank unless specifically required — normally folded into subcontractor labor cost.

Fees & commission: Commissions and Fees → Miscellaneous Fee dropdown (covers dumping/overhead/admin/contract fees — current cheat-sheet examples: 4% supply & install, 2% new construction, 2% supply only) → review gross profit margin against the printed Job Cost Breakdown (a large gap, e.g. BP showing 38% vs. JCB showing 48%, means something was missed) → Add Commission Sales per the commission cheat sheet/sliding scale (example: 10% on a window job) → check whether a credit card fee applies if payment was by Visa/Mastercard.

Baseline: Scope tab → Baseline → create new baseline named Estimated, set as Primary, Create Baseline. Any time a baseline updates, the newest one should be set Primary.

Physical file: return paperwork to the folder, write a brief product description on the front (e.g. "1 patio door, 3 windows"; list supplier per product if multiple manufacturers are involved), and complete the folder checklist (job breakdown = labor; cost breakdown = costing).

Final handoff: move the file to the Production Manager/designated ordering review area for a second set of eyes before the order is officially placed — the final order is not submitted at this stage.

Manufacturer Credits

The SOP for requesting and tracking manufacturer credits (damaged product, incorrect order, warranty issue) through to close, plus a real example of the follow-up cadence in practice from Production Meeting minutes.

Process: Requesting & Tracking Manufacturer Credits

Key rules: all manufacturer credits must be documented in Builder Prime; follow-ups are mandatory and time-based, not optional; a credit isn't complete until it's received, the client profile is updated, and the to-do is cleared; if a manufacturer is unresponsive, daily follow-up is required after the first escalation.

1. Identify the need: confirm the reason (damaged product, incorrect order, warranty issue) and gather documentation (photos, order number, invoice, notes).

2. Submit the request to the manufacturer per their process, with full supporting documentation to avoid delays.

3. Document in BP: add a Client Profile note — credit requested from [manufacturer], date requested, reason. Example: "Credit requested from KV on Jan 15, 2026 – damaged frame on rear patio door."

4. Create a follow-up to-do due 1 week from the request date ("Follow up on [manufacturer] credit request").

5. Follow-up timeline: no response after 3 days → begin daily follow-ups. At the 1-week to-do check, if still not received → follow up again and reset the to-do to 3 days later. Repeat this 3-day cycle until the credit is received.

6. Update notes with any status change, expected credit date, or delay communicated — brief but specific.

7. Close the credit: once received, add a final note (received, date, amount if applicable) and clear the to-do.

Real example — KV credit, TOWNS project Resolved

Production Meeting minutes show this process playing out in practice: an outstanding labor credit from KV for a defective window replacement on the TOWNS project was raised as New Business, with the Project Manager assigned to follow up on the pending reimbursement. It resurfaces in the next tracked meeting — "Town Credit — Communication and receipt of the Towns credit from KV. Received." — confirming the request-to-close cycle described in the SOP above actually closes out in real use.

Vendor Accounts & Purchase Orders

How CWD establishes credit accounts with suppliers, and the PO/invoicing convention seen across vendor documents and correspondence. There's no single written "vendor policy" doc — this is assembled from individual vendor files.

Vendor Credit & Account Applications

Per-vendor account/credit applications are filed under Operations/Vendors/[Vendor Name]/ (e.g. VDK Group) and OfficeManagement/Vendor Statements & Reports/ (e.g. Gentek). These are applications CWD submits as the applicant to establish a credit account with a new supplier.

Typical fields required: company name/address, nature of business, years in business, principal(s) name/title/home address, estimated monthly purchases, requested credit limit, banking info (name/branch/account), and business/trade references.

CWD's own application details (from the Gentek-filed credit app, dated Jan 2025): principals Serge Rochon and Marie Rochon, banking through RBC, requested credit limit range $35,000–$50,000, and trade references including KV Custom Windows & Doors, Simcoe Building Centre, and Door Hardware Supply.

Standard terms accepted: Net 30 days from invoice date, with a 2%/month (24%/year) service charge on overdue accounts — consistent across both the VDK Group and Gentek applications reviewed.

PO Format & Invoicing Convention

PO format used when CWD orders (per the Special Order Items process): Client Last Name + six-digit Builder Prime/CWD job number in the email subject line (e.g. SMITH 123456).

Net 30 + PO-numbered invoicing shows up consistently in vendor correspondence reviewed — e.g. a Kaycan Ltd. invoicing email referencing Customer PO No., Sales Order No., and Net 30 Days terms. A restoration-trade vendor relationship (Paul Davis Simcoe County, where CWD is the subcontractor/vendor side) mirrors the same convention in reverse: all vendor invoices must carry a PO number (each usable once), paid Net 30 with cheque runs every Friday — useful as a reference for the PO-based invoicing pattern CWD's own suppliers likely follow too, even though that specific document describes CWD as the payee, not the payer.
